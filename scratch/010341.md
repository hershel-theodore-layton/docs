This should round off the missing Ristretto functionality contained in the [PHP Sodium extension](https://www.php.net/manual/en/ref.sodium.php), which wasn't exposed. The missing stuff was point-related as opposed to scalar-related.

For what I am doing, I really only needed `sodium_crypto_core_ristretto255_add` but realized that I might as well add the other three things, especially because it makes testing easier.

 ---

*Read on if you are worried about why I am adding this, otherwise feel free to ignore*

As for what I need this for, **first of all my use case is non-crypto**. Before anyone gets worried.

What I am exploring is a way to get a collision-resistant ID from an unordered set. After some research, it seems this falls under the broader topic of "multiset hashing". The initial idea one might have is to just xor the hashes together, but that is actually broken for a whole lot of reasons, and the research into multiset hashing is basically trying to solve that problem without the pitfalls.

The newest research I can find utilizes elliptic curves on top of hash functions.

Specifically, I am following the (MIT licensed) Rust implementation described and linked in this post: https://cronokirby.com/posts/2021/07/on_multi_set_hashing/

*(I also saw a paper which I believe is possibly related: "Elliptic Curve Multiset Hash", Maitin-Shepard, Tibouchi, Aranha. However it is very dense, as is the linked code which is also GPL3 encumbered.)*

Anyway, to reiterate, my use is non-cryptographic. My hope is that the collision resistance promises hold, but it won't be a security issue if they don't.

Besides all that, I don't see any reason not to just finish including all the Ristretto stuff in HHVM, anyway.
