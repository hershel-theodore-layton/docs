## How does prefetching work?

To prefetch for a folding a decl `class D extends A,B,C {}` that's not already in folded cache, we start by looking at the shallow decl for D (either already in cache, or direct-decl-parsing off disk). This tells us that we'll also need folded decls for A,B,C, so we kick off these prefetches in parallel. Wide ancestor hierarchies are very amenable to parallelisation!

Observe that the algorithm repeatedly switches between cache-side work and direct-decl-parsing work:
1. Walk as far as we can in existing shallow+folded caches to find what shallow decls will be needed next
2. Resolve those to filenames
3. Kick off concurrent async direct-decl-parsing for those files
4. As soon as we got a direct-decl-parse from an earlier concurrent decl-fetch, store it in cache, and repeat from step 1

I was working for IDE benefits, where the caches are all stored in ocaml memory as HashTbl. I therefore figured it made most sense to implement step 1 in ocaml. If this same technique were to be used for the rust backend for hh_server then the equation might be different, but I never got to the bottom of what is the storage for those caches.

Step 2 could have been done either in Rust or Ocaml; it might have been nice to do naming-sqlite lookups concurrently on the rust side, rather than sequentially on the ocaml side, but that would be a bunch more engineering.

## Challenges

**Pessimisation**. When you fold a decl, the first step is to look up all the ancestors, and the second step is to dig through the return types of every method in the decl itself (not its ancestors) and judge whether they are enforced or not per the sound dynamic rules. Digging through the return type involves transitively resolving typedefs, and fetching further decls. Pessimisation contributes towards drastically higher numbers for "TRUE required decls". The precise algorithm for pessimisation is complicated. I really wanted a simple approximation. I iterated several times until I got something that was simple and had close enough agreement to the needs of pessimisation.

**Enums**. The folding rules for enums and enum classes are a bit complicated, in terms of which decls they need. For instance with `enum E {}` the shallow class decl for E indicates that it extends `\HH\BuiltinEnum`. To fold E, do we need the shallow or folded decl for this? If we subsequently want to look up an instance member on E, or do subtyping on it, how about then? What about `enum E : F as G`? And enum classes? I iterated several times until I got something that was simple and had close agreement to the needs of enum folding.
