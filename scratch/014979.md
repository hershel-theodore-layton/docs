Summary:
`Cursor`s are great, but they are quite bulky at 56 bytes. `ThinCursor` is light, but cannot support cloning.

My use-case needs to efficiently support **both** copying small many small byte ranges from a `Cursor`, **and** cloning pieces from a `Cursor`. The catch is that I need to store thousands (or more) of these references before I begin copying. Using `Cursor`  causes my storage to go from "2 items per cache-line" to "1 item per 2 cache-lines", and correspondingly performance on small values (copy) drops by up to 20%, while performance on large values (clone) drops by over 10%.

To be extra explicit: the above perf numbers compare storing `const IOBuf* buf; uint32_t offset; uint32_t size;` inline in my struct (which has 20 bytes of other data for a total of 32), versus storing a `Cursor` (which upsizes the struct to 80 bytes).

These buffer-level accessors on `Cursor` lets me sidestep this problem with minimal code, and performance essentially identical to storing just `const IOBuf*` and **not** using `Cursor` on the side that determines the boundaries of the data to be copied.
