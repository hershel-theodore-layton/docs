Commit title: Almost all await_suspend()s and await_ready()s are now noexcept
Commit body:
Summary:
Up-stack, we will use existing value-only wrappers -- `value_or_error...()` and `as_noexcept()` (soon to be renamed `value_or_fatal()`) in a more mission-critical setting.

Specifically, when they're being used for `async_closure` async RAII, and for `safe_async_scope`, it is *critical* that awaiting them **actually does not throw**.

As it turns out, all the wrappers today (including `co_awaitTry`) have a small hole in their non-throwing guarantee.
 - We already properly handle exceptions from `await_resume()`.
 - But, if `await_suspend()` or `await_ready()` throw at runtime, there is no viable "non-throwing" recourse -- the best you can do is terminate.

The value-only plumbing would therefore be *much* cleaner & safer if we couldjust assert that `await_suspend()` and `await_ready()` are `noexcept`. It turns out that:
  - Most of the these in `folly/` are already `noexcept`.
  - Most of the rest can safely be marked `noexcept`, simply by omission.
  - Only in two less-used awaitables -- in `fibers/Baton.h` and in `futures/Future.h` -- can these potentially throw. All their exceptions signal programmer error / precondition violations.

While it would be wonderful to blithely mark those `Baton` and `Future` functions as `noexcept`, there would be real production risk due to engineers who:
 - deliberately violate the precondition
 - catch the `logic_error` / `FutureAlreadyContinued` / or a catch-all.

I dug further, and the result was that:
 - `Future` **could** be fixed with low risk: D91284170
 - `fibers::Baton` does not need to be touched, because it lacks `await_resume_try` and `await_resume_result`, and thus is incompatible with value-only wrapper awaitables. Up-stack I added a `Baaton.h` comment to this effect.
