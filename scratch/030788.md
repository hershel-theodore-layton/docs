Summary:
(1) The instability criterion was too stringent -- at default 0.4%, I saw warnings like the below.  The first/second half estimates here do **not** fall in the other CI, but they're much closer than the target precision, which means we're just wasting time by waiting for the instability to resolve.

```
  coInvokeDirect: p33.30=2.8us to 0.02%, 9834 samples, 10s
    [unstable] 1st=2780.12 [2779.82, 2780.48] 2nd=2778.79 [2778.40, 2779.12]
```

**This change makes convergence faster, without sacrificing precision.**

(2) Use [random interleaving](https://google.github.io/benchmark/random_interleaving.html) instead of my previous round-robin.  In my quick checks, didn't really seem to have much effect, probably because the default slice is long enough.  But, it's ~free, and it makes sense to try to make the benchmarks encounter more of the dynamic-state variance of the machine.

(3) Put the "intermediate results" table AFTER the stats after all.  This is cleaner in the scenario where you have a long-running job -- the latest numbers update in-place at the bottom of the terminal.  Thanks to the annotations & indentation, they will not be confused with the final result.
